A checkout writes every file in one instant; these span 4.728 s with distinct
nanosecond values, and the three files edited during review carry minutes-later
times. The mtimes are curl artefacts.

they are precisely the two rows that produce the negative gaps in `run.log`.

## 3. Same-host gaps, measured two ways

| Measure | body mtimes (**authoritative**) | `run.log` `t0` (**superseded**) |
|---|---|---|
| same-host gaps measured | 21 | 21 |
| minimum gap | **+0.092 s** | -0.538 s |
| maximum gap | **+0.387 s** | +1.097 s |
| negative gaps | **0** | 2 |
| gaps >= 2.5 s (declared) | 0 | 0 |
| gaps >= 2.0 s (**AGENTS.md section 8 floor**) | **0** | 0 |
| gaps < 2.0 s — **rule breach** | **21** of 21 | 21 of 21 |

**No overlap.** Every mtime delta is positive and the 24 mtimes are strictly
increasing, so the 24 requests were issued sequentially and never concurrently.
The negative gaps in `run.log` are an artefact of two corrupt `date` reads, not
evidence of concurrent requests.

## 4. A third clock, and it is not ours

| Clock | Whose | Granularity | Inversions across the 24 rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Date:` response header | **Medtronic's server** | 1 s | **0** |
| body-file mtime | this machine's kernel | ns | **0** |
| `t0` in `run.log` | this machine's `date` | ms | **2** (seq 9, seq 20) |

Span of the server clock: 18:01:49 -> 18:01:54 UTC.

The response header is a record we captured for no timing purpose, written by
the far end, and it orders all 24 requests consistently with the local kernel
mtimes. Two clocks on two machines agree; only `run.log`'s `t0` dissents, and
only on the two rows that manufacture the negative gaps.
