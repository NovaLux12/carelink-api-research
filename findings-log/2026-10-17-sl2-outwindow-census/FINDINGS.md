## Method, and the rule that governs every cell

**There is no Oracle A/B/C for the CDX API, and this pass borrows none.**

- absence, record form → `404` + `{"message": "No Captures found for: …"}`
- `502` / `504` / `503` / `400` / `000` → **a failure, never an absence**, re-issued

**Counting rule, area L's, unchanged:**

> a cell reads as an absence **only if some row for that crawl answered `200`**.

A row counts as a *record* only when it parses as a JSON object carrying both
`url` and `timestamp`, so a `404` body can never inflate a count. The rule is
implemented in `derive.py --census`, not applied by eye.

**Controls, and what each one proved.**

| control | shape | rows | what it proved |
|---|---|---|---|
| positive, per crawl | `url=google.com&matchType=domain&limit=5` on that exact crawl | 57 attempts over 44 crawls | **43 of the 44** controls answered `200` on some attempt. **`CC-MAIN-2023-06`'s control did not** — seq 23 `502`, seq 24 `504`, seq 25 `504`, and no fourth attempt — but its **probe** answered `200` with 8 records at seq 26, so under F5's own rule (*no row of any kind answered `200`*) the crawl **is** served; it is a RECORD cell, and never an absence. The 43 answered controls cover the 43 other crawls, **including all four absence cells**, so **every** `404` in F5 still has a `200` control on its own crawl and **no crawl in the out-of-window set is unserved** |
| certainly-absent, **oldest** crawl | `url=zz-sl2-absent-20261017.minimed.eu&matchType=host` on `CC-MAIN-2008-2009` | seq 5 | **404**, 71 B — the absence oracle, established on the **2008** index |
| certainly-absent, **newest** crawl | same sibling on `CC-MAIN-2026-39` | seq 114 | **404**, 71 B — the oracle re-established at the other end of the sweep |

The sibling is a **fabricated name under a domain this sweep queries**. It was
never resolved, never sent to a nameserver, and is not a vendor asset.

**Why one control host and not a per-crawl alternation.** `google.com` was fixed
as the single control host **before** the sweep, because pool-L measured
`commoncrawl.org` first-attempt-failing on 4 of 7 crawls while `google.com`
covered all four (`findings-log/2026-10-14-pool-l-crawlcontrol/FINDINGS.md`).
Area L varied its control host per crawl and described it as "alternating"; its
round-1 correction C8 established it was not. One named host is one variable.

**The probe is unchanged from area L's shape:** `minimed.eu`,
`matchType=domain`, `output=json`, and **no `limit`** — because `limit` truncates
from the tail (`docs/10` §1). The largest probe response here is **6 027 B**, far
below the smallest whole-KiB boundary lane S6 measured (32 768 B), and **no probe
row sits on a 1024 boundary** (F7).

a 2014-and-2020 artifact, and it is not a 2022 artifact either**: it was being

## F8 — what `t_sent` actually is, and the one sub-floor gap

**Every prior pass in this area published a `t_sent` column and read it as "when
the request was sent". It is not.** In `probe.sh` the stamp is taken
*after* the `flock` line returns, and the `flock` line is
`sleep 3.5; curl …` — so `t_sent` is the moment the request **completed**, and
`t_done` follows it a few milliseconds later. The measured 113 gaps are therefore
**completion-to-completion** intervals, each ≥ 3.5 s by construction, and they
**overstate** the true send-to-send interval by at most the following request's
duration.

The property the delay rule exists to protect is **structural, not inferred from
timestamps**: the sleep is **inside** the lock, so two passes on this host cannot
both sleep and then send together. That holds by construction here and is the
claim worth making; the timestamp series bounds it and cannot prove it.

**One gap below the declared 3.5 s, and it is a corrupt stamp rather than a short
delay** — stated rather than smoothed, because this repo's record is that a green
spacing series is not the same as a faithful one (`docs/09` §4):

| clock | floor | under 3.5 s | negative durations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — `t_sent`** (harness clock, re-derivable from the committed ledger) | **3.339 s** | **1** (seq 44 → 45) | **1** |
| **B — kernel body-file mtime** (independent of the harness clock; **not** re-derivable from a clean clone, git does not preserve mtimes) | **3.992 s** | **0** | — |

Seq 44 (`CC-MAIN-2024-22` probe, `400 RemoteDisconnected`) has `t_done`
**0.892 s before** its `t_sent` — impossible under the harness's own code order,
and the **only** such row. Clock B measures that same pair at **4.236 s**, and
B's own floor across all 113 gaps is 3.992 s. **Both readings are published: either
the declared 3.5 s was honoured and one timestamp is wrong (clock B's evidence),
or it was not (clock A's).** No cause is asserted — `docs/09` §4's own precedent
is that a clock step, a truncation and a post-hoc edit are not separable from the
timestamps alone.

`spacing.txt` is the full 113-row series on both clocks, generated by
`spacing.py` from `results.jsonl` alone; clock B prints a **notice**, not a table,
when the body directory is absent, rather than silently printing nothing.
